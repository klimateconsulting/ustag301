U.S. Technical Advisory Group to

ISO/TC 301

Energy Management and Energy Savings

The U.S. Technical Advisory Group (TAG) to the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) Technical Committee (TC) 301, accredited by the American National Standards Institute (ANSI), is the organization authorized to represent the United States in the development of international standards on energy management and methods for determining energy savings which are published by ISO. The U.S. TAG participates in the activities of ISO/TC 301 and ensures U.S. national interests are fully represented. The U.S. TAG works with similar (i.e., “mirror”) committees from other countries as part of ISO/TC 301 – Energy Management and Energy Savings.

Standards

Significant Standards Produced by ISO/TC 301

ISO 50001:2018 – Energy Management

Energy management is practiced by many organizations, but strategic energy management (SEM) integrates energy performance into organizational strategy, senior leadership decision-making, and continual improvement processes. The internationally recognized standard for SEM is ISO 50001:2018 – Energy Management. It enables organizations to achieve such objectives as:

  • reducing energy costs,
  • improving energy efficiency and performance
  • verifying performance improvement, and
  • achieving business goals (e.g. sustainability).

ISO 50001 is designed so organizations using it can be audited and certified by independent parties. More than 88,000 sites worldwide are certified to ISO 50001, including over 2,000 in the United States. Certified organizations benefit from sustained energy performance improvements, with one study reporting average annual energy savings of approximately 4% per year over more than a decade. Organizations such as Microsoft, Charter Steel, Intel, 3M and Hilton have cited ISO 50001 as an important factor in achieving their energy and business goals. Companies report improved energy cost savings, improved productivity, positive effects on organizational culture, and enhanced public relations after becoming certified to ISO 50001.

ISO 50100:2026 – Using Energy Management to Manage Energy-Related Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Emissions

Published in March 2026, ISO 50100 provides voluntary guidance on how to use SEM to maximize competitiveness while meeting regulatory or market requirements to plan for and achieve long-term reductions in GHG emissions associated with energy consumption. ISO 50100 creates a fair and transparent framework to ensure that valid data and business practices are used to access competitive markets. Energy-related emissions—those that come from fuels an organization consumes itself or from electricity purchased from the grid—are the emissions most directly within an organization’s control. Examples include natural gas used in its boilers or diesel used in its vehicles. ISO 50100 can also be adapted to manage or reduce other sources of GHG emissions depending on an organization’s objectives. This standard establishes processes for collecting and maintaining data needed to accurately and fairly validate GHG reduction plans and to verify the effectiveness of actions taken to reduce energy-related GHG emissions. It also provides an excellent means for strategic energy partnerships in supply chains, enabling small and mid-sized organizations to actively participate in GHG emission reduction efforts.

The TAG

The Role of the U.S. TAG in Developing International Standards

Who’s On the U.S. TAG?

The U.S. TAG is composed of energy management experts from industry, government, academia, non-government organizations, and other organizations and individuals who contribute their time and expertise to the development of international standards useful to U.S. industry, commercial enterprises, and other organizations. U.S. TAG members help ensure that the U.S. perspectives are represented when global standards are developed, reviewed or revised. Drawing on experience across diverse sectors and organizations, members provide valuable insights that help shape practical and effective standards.

The U.S. TAG is administered by PDDD, LLC and chaired by Deann Desai.

Why Join the U.S. TAG?

U.S. TAG membership provides a unique opportunity to shape standards that influence energy management practices globally. Members have direct input in developing U.S. positions on standards as well as formulating responses to other countries’ proposals. Members also receive early access to pertinent ISO documents (subject to some restrictions), collaborate with leading energy management experts, and gain valuable professional development experience through participation in the international standards process.

U.S. TAG Members Responsibilities

The U.S. TAG follows the ANSI and ISO procedures for developing standards. U.S. TAG members review documents, provide comments and draft language, and vote on proposed standards at various stages of development. This process helps build consensus, which is the foundation of international standardization.

In addition to developing new standards, the U.S. TAG provides input on the revision, continuation, withdrawal, and interpretation of the standards under the scope of ISO/TC 301. The U.S. TAG also provides input on related documents from other ISO TCs that are working in the areas related to energy, energy efficiency, energy savings, emissions management, etc.

U.S. TAG members are required to follow a Code of Conduct and are expected to meet minimum participation requirements, including reviewing documents, providing comments, and voting on ballots. Additional information is available in the governance documents below. At present, there are no membership fees for the U.S. TAG to ISO/TC 301. Membership fees are under consideration and may be introduced in the future.

Looking Ahead

U.S. TAG’s Anticipated Future Work

Potential future areas of work for ISO/TC 301 include:

  • A mandatory review and revision of ISO 50001 – Energy Management.
  • Development of additional standards or guidance on integrating energy management and other business objectives.
  • Exploration of how advanced digital technologies, including artificial intelligence (AI), can support the implementation and continual improvement of energy management systems (EnMS) and standards.
  • Closer integration of EnMS into efforts to manage electricity demand growth, particularly growth associated with data centers.
  • Adjustment or development of energy management and auditing standards to align with regional and international efforts (e.g., the European Union).
How It Works

The Role of the U.S. TAG in the ISO Standard Setting Process

The U.S. TAG plays an essential role in ISO’s standards development process. When ISO member countries identify a need for a standard in a particular area or scope, a committee is organized to address that need. For example, ISO/TC 301 is responsible for standards in the scope of energy management and methods for determining energy savings. TCs must follow the ISO’s rules and procedures for their work to be published by the ISO as international standards.

National standards bodies, such as ANSI in the United States, which are interested in standards in a particular scope, organize a TAG of experts from that country to represent it in the TC responsible for that scope. The U.S. TAG to TC 301 is the designated TAG to represent the United States in TC 301.

A TAG must create and follow policies, rules, and procedures satisfying the requirements of its national standards body for it to be accredited and able to participate in a TC. For example, the U.S. TAG to TC 301 is accredited by ANSI and therefore able to represent the U.S. in TC 301.
