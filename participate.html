Participation FAQs

Join the U.S. TAG Developing International Standards for Energy Management

What participation in the U.S. Technical Advisory Group to ISO/TC 301 looks like, what members do, and how to get engaged.

The Basics

What is the TAG?

The U.S. TAG is the exclusive channel for U.S. input to the International Standards Organization (ISO) Technical Committee (TC) 301, developing international standards for energy management and methods for calculating energy saving. Members are instrumental in ensuring the TAG’s products benefit their users and surrounding communities.

What’s the TAG doing now?

  • We’re exploring new areas: demand flexibility and demand response, renewable energy in energy management, and AI and big data in energy measurement.
  • The ISO 50001 standard on energy management is now open for periodic revision. We’ll work on updates including how to incorporate renewable energy.
  • ISO 50100—using energy management to manage energy-related emissions—was just published. We’re going to work on certification criteria for it and also for the revised ISO 50001.

Who’s involved?

We are energy management experts from a range of backgrounds or organizations interested in energy management. Some members participate as individuals; others may be associated with their organization.

What is asked of TAG members?

Members actively contribute to standards as they are developed. We:

  • Help develop overall guidance and its application to specific standards,
  • Propose specific edits to improve a standard under development, and
  • Vote whether a standard advances through the standard development process.
Good to Know

What the TAG Is and Isn’t

  • The TAG is independent of the U.S. government, though some TAG members also work on federally funded projects,
  • The TAG follows the procedures of the ISO and the American National Standards Institute (ANSI)1 ensuring standards qualify for ISO publication,
  • Neither the TAG nor the ISO advocate specific technologies or policies,
  • In the U.S., TC 301 standards are used voluntarily; elsewhere governments have implicitly required them.2
There currently are no fees. To support future international efforts, the TAG is developing a fee structure for 2027; fees may vary by type of individual or organizational participation.

Other TAGs address other topics and have other administrators

  • An example: quality management standards (ISO 9001) are developed by TC 176 with U.S. input from a TAG administered by the American Society for Quality.

1. ANSI is an independent, non-profit organization and is the official representative for the U.S. to the ISO for developing international standards. More at: ansi.org/iso/us-representation-in-iso.
2. E.g. the EU’s “Energy Efficiency Directive” implicitly requires ISO 50001 for large energy-using organizations.
